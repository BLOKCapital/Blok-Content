## Script (as recorded; `build/script.txt`)
1. Shh. The man by the window? That's Isaac Newton.
2. London. June, 1720.
3. And he's about to lose a fortune. *(title card)*
4. Okay. Simple version.
5. Everyone in here is buying one stock. The South Sea Company.
6. In January, a share cost about a hundred and twenty-eight pounds. Now? It's heading for a thousand. *(ledger paper)*
7. Newton already sold his, back in April. Took a nice profit.
8. Then he watched his friends get richer.
9. So today, near the top, he's buying back in. With almost all his savings.
10. By September, it collapses. By December, a share's back to about a hundred and twenty-four pounds. *(ledger paper)*
11. The legend says he lost twenty thousand pounds. His records suggest closer to ten. Still, a third of everything he had. *(net-worth card)*
12. He wasn't dumb. Nobody here could see what was really underneath. They just watched the price.
13. So here's the idea.
14. Don't try to be smarter. Be more boring. Spread it out, keep costs low, and own what you can check.
15. Today, an on-chain portfolio sits on a public ledger. You can see every holding, and every move, yourself. *(ledger, Illustrative)*
16. It's newer, and prices can swing. So start small, and learn first. *(spoken risk line)*
17. You don't need to be a genius.
18. You just need to see what you own. *(end card)*

A line "This is the man who ran the Royal Mint." was recorded and cut in the edit for length (`DROP` in `build/stitch.py`). The famous "I can calculate the motions of the heavenly bodies, but not the madness of people" line is deliberately left out: it is a later attribution (see fact check).

## Fact check (sources and what each says)
| Claim on screen / in VO | Source | What the source says |
|---|---|---|
| Newton sold his South Sea stake in April 1720 at a profit | A. Odlyzko, "Newton's financial misadventures in the South Sea Bubble", *Notes and Records: The Royal Society Journal of the History of Science* 73(1), 2019, doi:10.1098/rsnr.2018.0018 (preprint: www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/mania13c.pdf) | Newton instructed sales of 30 shares on 19 April and 70 on 23 April 1720, liquidating all or nearly all his stake within about a week; most had been bought at 100 or below; he made a profit of about £20,000 in the early stages of the Bubble |
| He bought back in near the top, in June, with almost all his savings | Odlyzko 2019 | He re-entered after prices climbed from late May, moving essentially all his financial assets into South Sea stock, mostly in mid-June 1720, at "very high prices", "close to their peak"; kept buying until the September collapse |
| Lost about a third; ~£30,000 to ~£20,000; legend says £20,000 | Odlyzko 2019 | Net worth "somewhat over £30,000" shortly before the Bubble, "about £20,000" by mid-1721, "a loss of about £10,000"; "a famous anecdote claims he lost twice that much"; had he not reinvested he would have been worth over £50,000. Not ruined: his estate at death (1727) was about £30,000 |
| Newton ran the Royal Mint (cut from VO, kept as context) | Odlyzko 2019; Oxford "Newton & the Mint" | Master of the Mint 1699 until his death in 1727 |
| Jan 1720 ~£128 a share | Encyclopaedia Britannica, "South Sea Bubble" | Shares rose "from 128½ in January 1720" |
| Heading for £1,000 in June | University of Oxford, *Newton & the Mint*, "Newton and the South Sea Bubble" (newtonandthemint.history.ox.ac.uk); Britannica | Oxford: ~£950 by the end of June, peak £1,050. Britannica: "more than 1,000" by August. Hence "heading for a thousand" / "near £1,000" for June, not an exact peak date |
| Collapse by September; ~£124 in December 1720 | Britannica; Oxford | Britannica: "By September the market had collapsed... by December South Sea shares were down to 124". Oxford: fell to £190 in late summer, "£124 in December 1720" |
| "Nobody could see what was really underneath" | Britannica | The 1720 rise rested on the company's plan to take over the national debt and on "the foreseen rise in the value of its shares"; its actual trade was limited (one ship a year for general trade under the 1713 treaty). A Commons inquiry later found ministers had taken bribes |
| (left out) "madness of people" quote | Odlyzko 2019; Physics Today (Odlyzko, July 2020, doi:10.1063/PT.3.4521); Oxford | All three call it something Newton "supposedly"/"allegedly" said; Odlyzko notes the related anecdote has only been traced to about two generations after his death. Newton's own documented words (letter to Fatio): "I lost very much by the South Sea company" |
| Secondary, cross-check only | Smithsonian Magazine, "The Market Crash That Cost Newton a Fortune" (K. Eschner, 6 Jan 2017) | Repeats the popular version (£128 start, £1,050 peak, lost £20,000 citing Jason Zweig). We follow Odlyzko's primary-records figure instead and label £20,000 as the legend |

Could not verify: the exact price Newton paid in June (Odlyzko gives no single figure, only "close to their peak"); the exact peak date (sources differ: late June vs August), so the Reel avoids naming one.
