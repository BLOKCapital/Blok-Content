# Who Gets In First? Part 4 · script + hooks (draft, 16 Nov 2026)

## Angles considered (picked the sharpest)
1. **Customer vs owner (picked).** Jay (the recurring character from Part 3) eats at a taco truck every Friday for ten years. It becomes 300 restaurants. The owner's neighbors put money in on day one; Jay never could. Then the real fact: companies now stay private far longer before the public can buy in (median age at IPO about 9 years through the 1990s vs 12 in 2025). Why picked: everyone has a "favourite spot that blew up", it continues the series question (who gets in first), it uses a fresh, primary-sourced fact, and the customer/owner flip lands straight on BLOK's "own your own fund from $10".
2. Concert presale (fans who had a presale code got tickets first). Rejected: same mechanic as Part 2 (IPO allocations), would feel like a repeat.
3. Bulk-pack pricing (big buyers pay less per unit). Rejected: the money tie-in drifts into fees, which the brand narrative says not to attack.

## ICP + emotion
Working adults 22 to 40 who already put money into funds or a 401(k) and buy from brands they love. Emotion: the quiet sting of realising you helped something grow and got nothing for it (being a customer, never an owner). Story is fictional and labelled as such.

## Hook recommendations (hooks skill, `growth/hooks-skill/`)

**H1 (picked). Storytelling hook, in medias res with the twist first. "Loyal customer, owns nothing."**
- Spoken: "Jay bought tacos here every Friday for ten years. He owns none of it."
- Text overlay: word-synced captions (Plus Jakarta Sans ExtraBold), "ten years" in lime, "none" in coral.
- Visual: open tight on Jay at a small taco-truck window at dusk, string lights, a paper tray in his hand. A tiny "FRIDAY" tally on the truck counts up fast (1, 2, 3 ... 520). On "none of it" the camera drops back and the truck is now a giant glowing restaurant sign with "300 LOCATIONS".
- Sound: soft music bed, a tick for each tally, a low thud on "none".
- Vibe: warm, a little funny, then a small gut-punch.
- Tag: Iteration (storytelling format carried from Part 3; this sharpens it by leading with the twist).
- Why: the storytelling taxonomy says open in medias res and promise a perspective shift; leading with "owns none of it" sets the completion desire (how can that be?) in the first two seconds, and the loyal-customer moment is relatable to anyone with a favourite spot.

**H2. Question / curiosity-gap hook. "Who got rich off your favourite spot?"**
- Spoken: "Your favourite taco place made someone a lot of money. It wasn't Jay."
- Visual: Jay mid-bite, a cash register ringing on the truck counter, a shadowy neighbor in the window behind him.
- Tag: Untapped opportunity. Kept as a backup test.

**H3. POV relatability hook.**
- Text overlay: "POV: you were their first customer."
- Spoken: "Jay found this place when it was one window and a folding table."
- Tag: Untapped opportunity. Backup.

## Facts and sources
- Median age of companies at IPO: 9 years for 1990–1998 (Ritter's period row; yearly medians 8 to 10), 12 years in 2025 (14 in 2024; 1999 was 5, not used). Source: Jay Ritter, University of Florida, "Initial Public Offerings: Technology Stock IPOs", Table 4, updated 24 Dec 2025, https://site.warrington.ufl.edu/ritter/files/IPOs-Tech.pdf (all-IPO median age column; excludes SPACs, penny stocks, REITs etc.). "Typical" = median.
- Private company investments are mostly limited to accredited investors (SEC, Investor.gov: net worth over $1 million excluding home, or income over $200,000), which supports "mostly insiders and big money".
- Jay, the taco truck, ten years, 300 restaurants and the neighbors are made up (labelled in caption).
- BLOK: own fund from $10, user-controlled (self-custody) per CLAUDE.md / knowledge. No crypto words.
