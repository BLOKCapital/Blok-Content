in 1792, 24 men on wall street signed a deal named after a tree 🌳

the buttonwood agreement: they'd trade with each other first, and nobody would charge less than a fixed fee. that rule lasted 183 years, until 1 may 1975.

fees have fallen a lot since. the middlemen mostly stayed: a broker, a fund house, a custodian, someone holding your money for you.

buffett's advice for most people has always been simple: skip the expensive managers and own a cheap index fund.

now picture that same boring index with nobody in the middle holding your money. the rules are written in code, and anyone can check them.

how many middlemen sit between you and your money? count them in the comments 👇

follow @blok.capital for one plain-english money lesson a day.

sources: the Buttonwood Agreement, 17 May 1792 (0.25% minimum commission). The SEC ended fixed brokerage commissions on 1 May 1975 ("May Day"). Buffett's advice paraphrased from his Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letters.
