95 stocks. That's what a janitor who left $8 million owned. Part 2 of Ronald Read's story. 👇

In Part 1: Ronald Read pumped gas in Vermont for about 25 years, then worked 17 more as a part-time janitor. When he died in 2014, he left about $8 million, most of it to his local hospital and library.

So how? Before you read on, guess: hot tech stocks, or boring ones?

Boring ones. He owned at least 95 stocks across railroads, banks, utilities, healthcare and household names like toothpaste makers. He picked companies that paid dividends, reinvested those dividends, and held for decades. He stayed away from businesses he didn't understand. He kept the paper certificates in a safe-deposit box, about five inches thick.

Not every pick worked. He owned Lehman Brothers when it went bankrupt in 2008. With 95 stocks, one failure didn't sink him. That's diversification: spreading money so no single company can break you.
