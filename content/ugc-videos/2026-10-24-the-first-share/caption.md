The first share anyone could buy. 🌱

Amsterdam, August 1602. To pay for ships, the Dutch East India Company let ordinary people buy a piece of it. By the last night, 1,143 people had signed its book in Amsterdam alone, pledging about 3.7 million guilders. Nobody got a certificate. Your share was a line in the company's book, and only its clerk could change it. The oldest receipt ever found is from 1606: a city messenger, 150 guilders.

The simple lesson: 400 years on, owning a fund still works a lot like this. Your units are a line in someone else's records, and mostly that works fine. An on-chain portfolio flips it: the book is public, anyone can read it, and only you can sign your own line. It's newer and prices can swing, so start small and learn first.

If you could read your fund's book yourself, what's the first thing you'd check? 👇
