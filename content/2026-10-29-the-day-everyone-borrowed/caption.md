Money history, part 2: the day everyone borrowed 📉

October 1929. Ordinary people were finally buying stocks. The catch? Many put down just 10% and borrowed the rest. When prices crashed on Black Tuesday (29 October), the loans came due and many lost everything.

Five years earlier, a Boston fund had quietly done something different: it let ordinary people in, and let them sell back any time at the real value of what the fund held.

The lesson still holds. Getting in isn't enough. Own what you buy, and be able to see exactly what you hold.
