The day the phones stopped answering. 🌱

Monday, 19 October 1987. The Dow fell 22.6% in a single day, and 604 million shares traded on the New York Stock Exchange. Many small investors couldn't get through to their brokers, and some market makers simply stopped answering the phone. Stocks did recover, back to their pre-crash highs in under two years. But that day, a lot of people couldn't even find out where they stood.

The simple lesson: when it matters most, you want to be able to see what you own. An on-chain portfolio sits in an account only you control, on a public ledger that's open around the clock and anyone can check. It's newer and prices can swing, so start small and learn first.
