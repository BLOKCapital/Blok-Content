money history, part 1: the first fund ever made was for people without much money.

Amsterdam, 1774. In what is widely seen as the first mutual fund, a broker named van Ketwich pooled savings from lots of people so that "investors of limited means" could spread their money across many investments instead of betting it all on one. He called the fund Eendragt Maakt Magt: unity is strength.

250 years later, the fund that's built around you has a door policy. In India, a portfolio managed for you (PMS) starts at ₹50 lakh. An alternative fund (AIF) starts at ₹1 crore. Even the new specialised funds (SIF) ask for ₹10 lakh. Then come the setup fees and the bank's introduction fee.

Plenty of these funds are run well, and what they charge for running them is fair. The problem is the door. Most people never get in.

We think everyone should have a fund of their own, even a $10 one. An on-chain portfolio is exactly that: your own basket of investments, in an account only you control, starting from $10.
