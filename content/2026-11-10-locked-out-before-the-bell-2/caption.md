Money works nights now. So why does your fund still wait for the closing bell? 👇

In the 1960s, John Shepherd-Barron got to his bank just after closing and couldn't get his own money out. Later, in the bath, he thought: if a machine can sell a chocolate bar, why not cash? In June 1967, Britain's first cash machine opened outside a Barclays branch in Enfield.

This week Canada switched on its Real-Time Rail: payments that clear and settle in seconds, 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

But most US mutual funds still set one price a day, at the 4 p.m. close. Put money in at 9 p.m. and you get the next trading day's price. And having a fund of your own usually starts at $10,000 or more.

A fund on a public ledger has no closing bell. What it holds trades around the clock, and you can check it any time. Here's the catch, though: around the clock isn't a reason to trade more. Good long-term plans don't change at 9 p.m. You're just never locked out.
