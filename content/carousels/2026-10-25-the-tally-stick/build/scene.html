England · 1100s to 1826

For 700 years, Britain's receipts were made of wood.

notched sticks.
Lend money to the king and the amount was cut into a stick of hazel. The wider the notch, the bigger the sum.
The clever part

Then they split the stick in two.

so both sides had proof.
The lender kept the longer half, the stock. The borrower kept the foil. If the halves matched, the record was true. It's where "stock" in finance comes from.
16 October 1834

Then they burned the old sticks.

and lost Parliament.
Tallies were scrapped in 1826. Years of leftover sticks went into the furnaces under the House of Lords. The fire spread and burned down most of the Palace of Westminster.
