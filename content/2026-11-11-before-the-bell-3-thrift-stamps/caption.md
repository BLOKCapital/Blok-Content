In December 1917, one US War Savings stamp cost $4.12. Too much, all at once, for a lot of families. So the Treasury also sold a 25 cent Thrift stamp. Fill a card with 16, add a few cents, and you could trade it for a War Savings stamp worth $5 by January 1923.

This month, one of the year's biggest AI listings is reportedly weighing setting aside 15 to 20% of its IPO shares for regular people. That's the headline: regular people, maybe, getting a slice. The plan is still being discussed, and when demand is high, most people get a small, pro-rated amount.

Funds work the same way. If you want a fund of your own, run just for you, it often starts at $10,000 or more.

In 1917 the fix was a 25 cent stamp. Today it can be a fund on a public ledger: every holding and every trade on record, and you can start small.
