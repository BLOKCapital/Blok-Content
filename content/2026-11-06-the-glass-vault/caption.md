In 1954, a New York bank put its vault in the front window. Here's why that wasn't crazy 👇

The Manufacturers Trust building at 510 Fifth Avenue opened with its round steel vault door (about 30 tons) at street level, behind glass, where anyone walking past could see it. Seeing a vault and opening it are two different things.

Money on-chain works the same way. Balances sit on a public ledger, so anyone can look. Moving the money takes the private key, a secret code that should never leave your hands. That's why thieves go after people, not the vault: fake "support" messages, look-alike websites, and requests to approve a transfer.

3 rules to keep the key yours:
1. Never share your recovery phrase (your secret backup words). No real company will ever ask for them.
2. Write it on paper and keep it offline. No screenshots.
3. Keep your account address away from your real name online.
