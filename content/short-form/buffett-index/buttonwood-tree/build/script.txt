In 1792, twenty-four men on Wall Street signed a deal named after a tree.
They'd give each other first dibs on trades. And nobody would charge less than a fixed fee.
That fee rule lasted a hundred and eighty-three years. Until 1975.
Fees have fallen a lot since. But the middlemen never really left.
A broker. A fund house. A custodian. Someone, somewhere, holding your money for you.
Warren Buffett's advice for most people? Skip the expensive managers. Own a cheap index fund.
Now picture that same boring index, with nobody in the middle holding your money. The rules are written in code, and anyone can check them.
How many middlemen sit between you and your money? Count them in the comments.
